Nib math

Every calligraphy guideline is the nib width times a count. Five nib widths of x-height on a 2 mm nib is 10 mm - and the whole page falls out of that one multiplication.

Rule the page first

The problem it solves

Beginners rule guidelines by eye and wonder why the letters drift. The craft's actual unit is the nib width: x-height, ascender space and descender space are all counted in nibs, so a change of pen silently changes every line on the page. A 5-nib x-height is 7.5 mm on a 1.5 mm nib and 12.5 mm on a 2.5 mm nib - same hand, different ruler.

Nib math turns the count into millimeters: the guideline ladder for your pen and hand, how many full lines actually fit between your margins (and the awkward gap at the bottom), the horizontal offset of a slanted guideline, and a nib ruler that measures any distance in nib widths.

What it does not do

The arithmetic is exact; the craft labels are not law. The compressed / mid / airy bands and the classic slant zone are flagged craft norms, and hands vary. It rules the page - it does not move the pen.
